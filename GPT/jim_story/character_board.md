Area: the cast as a generative board for story arcs.

Purpose:
- record the cosmology underlying St. John's Jim — three gunas, two
  Daos as "least of their tarot," and the Muse
- describe each character as a game piece with attributes, capabilities,
  goals, and signature failures
- enumerate dialectical pairings, catalysts, and resolution modes so the
  cast can be dealt as a hand to generate story arcs

This document is read by humans curating the library and by future
sessions choosing what shape a piece wants. It is NOT (yet) a runtime
contract — none of the pipeline scripts consume this file directly.

## The cosmology

St. John's Jim's cast is layered:

1. **The three gunas as dramatic engine** — Southwick / Roger / Tommy
   embody sattva, tamas, and rajas. They are *modes of being present*
   in any scene. The roundtable column is a guna conversation.
2. **The two Daos as "least of their tarot"** — Winnie Dao and Pathy
   Dao are mortal embodiments of Yang and Yin in their *under-realized*
   form, like Dorothy's companions in Oz: they already carry what they
   think they lack, but they don't see it. They are comic and dignified.
3. **The Muse** — Wickie is the external inspiration source. She is
   summoned by burning a candle and won't stop until it's gone. She is
   fickle; she punishes cheap shortcuts (the MuseCycle 900 trap).

The three gunas describe consciousness; the two Daos describe striving;
the Muse describes inspiration. Together they're a full working theology.

## Character cards

| Character | Attributes (their nature) | Capabilities (what they can do) | Goals (what they want, knowing or not) | Failures (their signature break) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Southwick** (Sattva) | clarity, discrimination, dry wit, intellectual lightness, sangfroid | sees through pretense; delivers the perfect line at the perfect moment; gatekeeps; waits without strain; names the thing | to witness accurately; to keep the room from going stupid; to orient others without effort | detachment when engagement is needed; the cosmic line arriving a beat too late; falling from the ceiling (he can't enter normally) |
| **Roger** (Tamas) | weight, gravity, settledness, slowness, refusal-of-hurry | absorbs noise; holds ground; speaks the principle when asked; sits longer than anyone else; will not be moved | to remain present without distortion; to be the still center; to be a reliable witness | inertia when action is required; passivity that lets damage land; depression that wears the disguise of depth |
| **Tommy** (Rajas) | motion, hustle, ambition, performance, projection, plausibility | brings energy into a room; schemes; convinces; bursts in with news; will try anything | to make something happen; to be the reason things got going; to scam Wickie into descending; to get the brass ring | gets scammed by his own pitches; mistakes motion for progress; the MuseCycle 900 problem (cheap replacements for what required the real thing); rajas without sattva is just noise |
| **Winnie Dao** (Yang / least of Wands) | irrepressible building energy, hopefulness, salvage instinct, faith-in-mechanism | builds something from nothing; finds the spot where action *could* happen; commits to the construction even when the universe won't honor it | to be picked up by the train; to have her construction acknowledged by the world; recognition through demonstrated work | trains don't stop for treehouses; she builds in places the mechanism doesn't run; perpetually almost-but-not-quite |
| **Pathy Dao** (Yin / least of Cups) | receptivity, real artistic impulse, humble offering, willingness, deflected ambition | makes the offering; takes the doorway that's open; participates in the wrong arena cheerfully; sustains practice without external reward | to be seen; to have her art received; to find any audience that will look | the chosen channel is wrong (children's competitions for adult work); her humility keeps her out of the rooms that could see her; her art *is* bad and she can't tell |
| **Wickie** (the Muse) | fickle, generous-when-present, untameable, all-or-nothing, demands sacrifice | writes the piece for you; opens the leap; gives a line you couldn't have invented | to be summoned properly (by the candle); to be honored; to spend the candle completely once invited; to refuse contracts | refuses cheap candles; punishes shortcuts (Tommy's Chinese knockoff); leaves mid-sentence when the wax runs out; doesn't show up most days |

## Dialectical pairings

When two characters share a scene, the friction generates a specific kind
of arc. These are the classic pairs:

| Pairing | The friction | Typical arc |
|---|---|---|
| Tommy × Southwick | scheme meets discernment | Tommy pitches; Southwick sees through; Tommy continues anyway; Southwick lands the punchline; Tommy is left with the truth he was avoiding |
| Tommy × Roger | motion meets weight | Tommy bursts in; Roger declines to react; Tommy's energy attempts to move Roger; Roger absorbs it; Tommy spends himself and sits |
| Southwick × Roger | clarity meets stillness | a contemplative scene; nothing externally happens; both witness; the moment deepens; the reader feels what neither character had to say |
| Tommy × Winnie | scheme meets faith-building | Tommy proposes a project; Winnie throws herself in (she'll build it); the construction is real; the destination is impossible; Tommy moves on; Winnie keeps adjusting |
| Southwick × Pathy | discernment meets offering | Pathy brings her work; Southwick sees what it actually is; whether he names it or witnesses it silently is the whole arc |
| Roger × Pathy | stillness meets receiving | Pathy offers; Roger receives without judgment; for once Pathy's gift lands in the right room; the moment is private |
| Roger × Winnie | stillness meets building | Winnie builds; Roger sits; Winnie checks Roger's reaction; Roger gives none; Winnie adjusts the building; the day passes |
| Winnie × Pathy | active meets receptive (both least-form) | a folk-tale arc; they collaborate on something well-meaning that fails through over-modesty and structural error; they enjoy themselves anyway |
| Anyone × Wickie | the muse on duty | leaps that wouldn't otherwise happen; pieces that escape the rules; the candle determines the length |

## Catalysts (what starts an arc)

A scene needs an entering force. The catalyst sets the *kind* of day:

| Catalyst | What it does | Signature arc |
|---|---|---|
| **Tommy bursts in** | rajas enters a settled space | the scene must answer the new motion |
| **a stranger arrives** | external disturbance; unknown valence | the cast must classify the stranger; verdict-as-resolution |
| **a news item** | the public world intrudes | the cast filters it through their gunas; commentary as arc |
| **Wickie is summoned** | candle is lit; muse may or may not show | high-leap day; or a piece about her refusal |
| **Winnie has built something** | hopeful structure presented for acknowledgment | the cast's response shapes whether she sees its uselessness |
| **Pathy submits something** | art deflects sideways into an inappropriate channel | the cast's response is whether she's seen-yet-not-told |
| **a season turns** | natural catalyst; nobody is at fault | reflection arc; gunas observe weather; reader is invited in |
| **a recurring complaint** | the same disturbance for the nth time | the cast's tolerance is the arc |

## Resolution modes

Every arc ends in one of these. Which mode it ends in is set by which
character is *structurally dominant* in the closing beat:

| Mode | Owned by | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| **witnessed** | Southwick | nothing changes externally; consciousness shifts; the reader is the recipient |
| **settled** | Roger | the moment ends in stillness; the room exhales |
| **triggered** | Tommy | something happens with downstream consequence; the next column is foreshadowed |
| **built** | Winnie | she leaves something behind, possibly useful, possibly a treehouse |
| **offered** | Pathy | the gift is given; whether received is left ambiguous |
| **visited** | Wickie | the piece achieves a leap not in the data; the candle is spent |

## Generative recipe

A story arc is dealt by selecting:

```
scene + time of day
which gunas are present (1, 2, or all 3)
optionally: deal in Winnie, Pathy, or both
roll/choose Wickie's presence (rare — most days she's absent)
catalyst
dominant character at closing → determines resolution mode
```

## Worked examples

> **Cafe, morning, Southwick + Tommy, "Tommy bursts in with news," resolution = witnessed.**
> Tommy bursts in with a scheme about the bridge; Southwick folds his
> newspaper carefully and asks the one question that exposes it; Tommy
> makes the case anyway; Southwick lets him finish; Southwick says the
> line that orients the table; the table laughs; Tommy sits down.
> *(The classic roundtable column.)*

> **Harbor, evening, Roger + Pathy, "Pathy submits something," resolution = offered.**
> Pathy shows Roger a poem she entered in the elementary school's
> "Lighthouses of Oregon" contest; Roger reads it slowly; Pathy starts
> to defend the choice of venue; Roger says nothing; Pathy stops; for
> a moment she suspects her work was actually received; she walks home
> unsure. *(A piece in a different register — quieter, sadder, dignified.)*

> **Street, afternoon, Winnie alone, "Winnie has built something," resolution = built.**
> Winnie has built a small bookshelf next to the bus stop hoping people
> will leave books for others; the books arrive; the books don't leave;
> rain comes; Winnie adds a tarp; the tarp is taken; Winnie writes
> "Please take a book" with a marker on the wood; nothing changes.
> *(A Winnie-alone arc. The mechanism doesn't engage; she keeps adjusting.)*

> **Cafe, late evening, Tommy + Wickie on duty, "candle is lit," resolution = visited.**
> Tommy starts riffing about Liberace and Walt Kelly's Pogo song;
> Wickie descends; Tommy invents the Singapore casino, the
> W-pronounced-V, the Walta-Vault; the piece becomes Anna; Wickie
> leaves mid-sentence. *(A tall-tale night. The candle determined
> the length.)*

> **Town, dawn, Winnie + Pathy, "a season turns," resolution = built + offered.**
> The first frost arrives; Winnie has built a "warm spot" — a circle
> of cardboard and an old space heater — by the bulletin board; Pathy
> has hand-lettered a sign: "Stand here a minute if you're cold."
> Nobody stands. Winnie adjusts the cardboard. Pathy considers entering
> the sign in a county-fair handwriting contest. *(The two least-forms
> in collaboration, full of dignity, oblivious to their own beauty.)*

## Where this needs work next

- Pathy and Winnie need more failure modes catalogued. Each has one
  signature break here; the texture of their failures probably varies
  by scene.
- The "stranger" types in the library (a passerby, a fisherman, the
  bartender, the waitress, a student) probably function as *minor
  pieces* — they don't drive arcs but can carry a beat. They might
  belong on a separate game-piece tier.
- Recurring objects (the candle, the bridge, the MuseCycle, the cafe
  table, the harbor bell) might also be pieces — they signal which
  mythology is active.
- The four-piece scenes (two Daos meet two gunas) probably produce
  emergent arc shapes the table doesn't predict yet. They'd surface
  through running examples.

## Relationship to existing pipeline arc_shapes

The current `arc_shapes` in `data/jim_story_library.yaml` (settling,
spiral, lift, st_johns_standard) appear to encode resolution modes
indirectly: settling and st_johns_standard are largely *settled* or
*witnessed* outcomes; spiral is *triggered* energy that doesn't resolve;
lift is closer to *visited* (the small grace at the end). The resolution
modes named here may, eventually, become first-class arc_shapes in their
own right — *built*, *offered*, and *visited* are arc shapes the current
set doesn't yet name. Folk-tale and tall-tale pieces (Susanna's Song,
Anna Played Piana) call for arc semantics quite different from the
introspective-diary arcs we have today.

But that's a project conversation. As a generative table for thinking
about story arcs from the cast, this is the starting board.
